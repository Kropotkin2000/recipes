Curry Base (Approx. 1 Liter Yield)
Yields: Approximately 1 Liter of Curry Base
Prep time: 25-35 minutes (including cashew soaking)
Cook time: ~ 45-55 minutes + cooling time
________________


Ingredients:
* Aromatics & Base:
   * Red Onion: 500g (approx. 2-3 medium onions)
   * Tomato: 375g (approx. 3-4 medium tomatoes)
   * Garlic: 35-40g (approx. 7-10 cloves)
   * Ginger: 20g (approx. 1 inch piece, peeled)
   * Green Chili: 1 (adjust to your spice preference)
   * Cashew Nuts: 70g (soaked in warm water for at least 30 minutes)


* Whole Spices (Bloom/Tempering):
   * Ceylon Cinnamon Sticks: 1 (ensure it's Ceylon)
   * Green Cardamom: 4
   * Black Cardamom: 1
   * Black Peppercorn: 1 (or 2 if you prefer)
   * Cloves: 4
   * Star Anise: 1/2 (or 1 very small one)
   * (Optional: 1 European Bay Leaf if you want a leaf element, otherwise omit Tej Patta as discussed)


* Ground Spices:
   * Cumin: 1/2 tbs (1.5 tsp)
   * Turmeric: 1/2 tbs (1.5 tsp)
   * Coriander: 1/2 tbs (1.5 tsp)
   * Red Chili Powder: 1/2 tbs (1.5 tsp) (adjust to your spice preference)
   * Garam Masala: 1/2 tsp


* Fats:
   * Oil: 200ml total (Vegetable, Sunflower, or Canola oil)
      * 100ml for Part 1
      * 100ml for Part 2
   *    * Vegan Butter: 1 tbs


* Sweetener & Seasoning:
   * Brown Sugar: 1/2 tbs (1.5 tsp)
   * Salt: 1/2 tbs (1.5 tsp) (adjust to taste - start with less)


* Liquid:
   * Water: As needed (for spice paste and adjusting consistency)


Instructions:
Part 1: Building the Aromatic Base
1. Bloom Spices: Heat 100ml of oil in a medium-large, heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the Ceylon cinnamon stick, green cardamom, black cardamom, star anise (half), cloves, and peppercorn (and European Bay Leaf, if using). Cook for 2-3 minutes, or until fragrant. Be careful not to burn.
2. Aromatics: Add the minced garlic and cook for 1-2 minutes, until lightly golden. Add the grated or finely minced ginger and cook for another minute.
3. Onions: Add the chopped red onions and salt. Cover the pot and cook on low heat for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onions are softened and lightly browned.
4. Remove Whole Spices (Optional for some): Remove the cinnamon stick, black cardamom, and star anise piece. (You can leave the smaller cloves and green cardamom if you don't mind them being blended, or remove them too).
5. Chili & Cashews: Add the chopped green chili and the soaked and drained cashew nuts.
6. Tomatoes: Add the chopped tomatoes. Cover and cook on low heat for 15-20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tomatoes are very soft and broken down.
7. Cool & Blend: Remove the pot from the heat and let the mixture cool completely. Transfer the cooled mixture to a blender and blend until very smooth. Add a little water if needed to help the blending process. Set aside.
Part 2: Finishing the Curry Base
1. Spice Paste: In a bowl, combine the cumin, turmeric, coriander, and red chili powder with enough water to form a smooth paste.
2. Tempering Spices: Melt the vegan butter and the remaining 100ml of oil in a clean, large pot over medium heat. Add the spice paste and cook for 1 minute, stirring constantly, until fragrant.
3. Combine & Simmer: Add the blended tomato-onion-cashew base to the pot with the cooked spice paste. Add the brown sugar. Stir well to combine.
4. Simmer: Cover the pot and cook on low heat for 15-20 minutes, stirring occasionally, to allow the flavors to meld. Adjust the consistency with a little water if needed.
5. Final Touches: Stir in the garam masala. Taste and adjust salt as needed.

Madras Curry
Yields: Varies depending on amount of veg/protein (approx. 2-4 servings)
Prep time: 15-20 minutes (assuming base sauce and cooked veg/protein are ready)
Cook time: 20-30 minutes
________________


Ingredients:
Part 1: For the Enriched Tomato Paste (to be made first or as you go)
* Plain Tomato Paste: 1 "heaped chef's spoon" (approx. 2-3 tbsp)
* Vegan Butter: 1 "knob" (approx. 1 tbsp)
* Ground Fenugreek: 1 pinch (or finely crushed fenugreek seeds)
* Chaat Masala: 1 pinch
Part 2: For the Curry
* Spiced Oil from your "Indian Curry Base Sauce": 1-2 tbsp (or plain oil if not available)
* Dried Fenugreek Leaves (Kasuri Methi): 1 pinch
* Fresh Ginger: Approx. 1 inch piece, finely chopped
* Garlic Cloves: 2-3, minced
* Fresh Green Chilies: 1-2, slit or chopped (optional, adjust to taste)
* Enriched Tomato Paste: The "heaped chef's spoon" made in Part 1
* Water: As needed for consistency
* Turmeric Powder: 1/2 tsp
* Kashmiri Red Chili Powder: 1/2 tsp (for color and mild heat)
* Coriander Powder: 1/2 tsp
* Ground Cumin: 1/2 tsp
* Ground Black Pepper: 1/2 tsp
* Pre-Cooked Vegetables of Choice: e.g., potatoes, peas, cauliflower, carrots, bell peppers
* Pre-Cooked Protein of Choice: e.g., tofu, chickpeas, seitan, lentils
* Fresh Coriander (Cilantro): A pinch, chopped (for cooking) + more for finishing & garnish
* Fresh Green Chilies: 1, finely chopped (for cooking, if desired for extra fresh heat)
* Indian Curry Base Sauce (from previous recipe): 1 "heaped chef's spoon" (approx. 2-3 tbsp, or more to taste)
* Salt: 1/2 tsp (or to taste)
* Full-Fat Coconut Cream: 1.5 tbsp
* Chaat Masala: 1/2 tsp (for finishing)
* Fresh Ginger: Thin strips for garnish
Instructions:
Step 1: Prepare the Enriched Tomato Paste (if not doing it directly in the curry pan)
* "Tomato paste is made from cooking down tomatoes in a knob of vegan butter with a pinch of fenugreek and chaat masala." If you are making it from scratch from fresh tomatoes, that's a longer process.
* If you're enriching existing plain tomato paste: In a very small pan or on the side, gently heat the plain tomato paste with the knob of vegan butter, pinch of ground fenugreek, and pinch of chaat masala until combined and fragrant. Set aside. (Alternatively, you can do this directly in the main curry pan before adding other ingredients if you prefer fewer dishes).
Step 2: Build the Curry
1. Heat the "Spiced Oil from the Base Sauce" (or plain oil) in a medium-sized pot or karahi over medium heat.
2. Once the oil is warm, scrunch in the pinch of dried fenugreek leaves (Kasuri Methi) and let them sizzle for a few seconds.
3. Add the chopped fresh ginger and cook for about 30 seconds until aromatic.
4. Add the minced garlic and cook for another 30-60 seconds until fragrant (do not brown).
5. If using, add the optional slit/chopped green chilies and cook for a minute.
6. Add the "heaped chef's spoon" of your Enriched Tomato Paste (from Part 1 or made directly in the pan). Cook for 1-2 minutes, stirring, allowing it to deepen in color and cook out its raw flavor.
7. Add a small splash of water if the paste becomes too thick, to prevent sticking and help it incorporate.
8. Stir in the turmeric powder, Kashmiri red chili powder, coriander powder, ground cumin, and ground black pepper. Cook for 30-60 seconds, stirring, until the spices are fragrant.
9. Add a little more water as required to form a thick sauce base.
10. Add your pre-cooked vegetables and pre-cooked protein of choice. Stir gently to coat everything with the spice paste. Cook for 2-3 minutes.
11. Add the pinch of chopped fresh coriander and the finely chopped fresh green chili (if using for extra fresh heat). Cook for another minute.
12. Add the "heaped chef's spoon" of your Indian Curry Base Sauce. Stir well to combine and cook for 2-3 minutes, allowing the flavors to meld.
13. Add the salt and stir.
14. Continue to cook the curry, stirring occasionally, until the oil begins to visibly separate slightly at the edges of the sauce (this indicates the sauce is well cooked). This might take 5-10 minutes. Add small splashes of water if it becomes too thick.
15. Stir in the 1.5 tablespoons of coconut cream. Cook for another minute until just heated through and combined.
16. Turn off the heat. Stir in the 1/2 teaspoon of chaat masala and the large pinch of fresh coriander for finishing.
Step 3: Garnish and Serve
1. Garnish the curry with more fresh coriander and the thin strips of fresh ginger.
2. Serve hot with rice, roti, naan, or your preferred accompaniment.
Notes:
* "Heaped chef's spoon": This is a subjective measure, generally meaning a generous spoonful, likely equivalent to 2-3 standard tablespoons. Adjust to your preference.
* "Spiced oil from the base sauce": If your Indian Curry Base Sauce has oil separating at the top, using this will add an extra layer of flavor.
* Pre-cooked Components: This recipe assumes your vegetables and protein are already cooked to nearly tender, as it focuses on building the sauce and combining.
* Adjusting Consistency: Add water in small amounts as needed throughout the cooking process to achieve your desired sauce consistency.
* Taste and Adjust: Always taste at the end and adjust salt, chili, or chaat masala if needed.

Ingredients
* 300 g strong white bread flour
* 6 g salt
* 6 g baking powder
* 1 tsp sugar (optional)
* 90 g plant-based Greek-style yoghurt
* 120–140 g warm water
* 25 g melted vegan butter or neutral oil
* Neutral oil, for greasing
* Extra flour, for dusting
Method
Mix flour, salt, baking powder and sugar.
Add yoghurt and melted vegan butter. Gradually add warm water to form a soft dough.
Knead 5–8 minutes until smooth, then rest covered for 30–60 minutes.
Divide into 6–8 balls and roll into ovals or teardrops.
Cook in a very hot dry pan until bubbles form and underside is browned, then flip and cook the other side.
Brush with melted vegan butter or oil and serve.

Tofu "Beef" Mince
This recipe uses a powerful combination of savory ingredients and a simple baking method to transform firm tofu into a remarkably convincing and delicious substitute for ground beef. It's perfect for use in classic dishes like bolognese, chili, or tacos.
Yields: Approx. 2 cups (enough to substitute for ~1 lb / 450g of ground beef)
Prep time: 10 minutes
Cook time: 20-30 minutes
________________


Ingredients:
* 300g firm or extra-firm tofu, crumbled
* 2 tbsp neutral oil
* 2 tbsp soy sauce (or tamari)
* 2 tbsp nutritional yeast
* 2 tbsp water
* 1 tsp Marmite
* 1 tsp garlic powder
* 1 tsp smoked paprika
* 1/2 tsp liquid smoke
* 1/2 tsp Maggi seasoning
* 1/2 tsp onion powder
* 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce (ensure it's a vegan version)
________________


Instructions:
Step 1: Prepare the Tofu and Oven
* Preheat your oven to 175°C (350°F).
* Line a large baking sheet with parchment paper.
* Press the tofu to remove as much water as possible. This is crucial for achieving a "meaty," chewy texture. Crumble the pressed tofu into a medium bowl. The crumbles can be irregular in size, just like real ground meat.
Step 2: Create the "Beef" Marinade
* In a separate bowl or measuring cup, combine all other ingredients: the oil, soy sauce, nutritional yeast, water, Marmite, garlic powder, smoked paprika, liquid smoke, Maggi, onion powder, and Worcestershire sauce.
* Whisk vigorously until the Marmite is completely dissolved and the marinade is smooth.
Step 3: Marinate the Tofu
* Pour the marinade over the crumbled tofu.
* Gently but thoroughly toss with a spatula until every piece is evenly coated.
Step 4: Bake to Perfection
* Spread the marinated tofu in a single, even layer on the prepared baking sheet.
* Bake for 20-30 minutes. For a softer mince ideal for sauces (like bolognese), aim for 20-25 minutes. For a firmer, more browned mince for tacos or chili, bake for the full 30 minutes.
* Stir the crumbles at the halfway point to ensure they cook evenly.
* The tofu "mince" is ready when it's browned, chewy, and the liquid has been fully absorbed.
________________


How to Use Your Tofu "Beef" Mince:
Use this exactly as you would use cooked ground beef. It's already seasoned, so you may want to adjust the salt in your main recipe accordingly.
* Spaghetti Bolognese: Sauté onion, carrots, and celery, then add the baked tofu mince and your favorite tomato sauce.
* Chili Sin Carne: Add it to your pot of beans, tomatoes, and chili spices.
* Tacos & Nachos: Use it as the filling for hard or soft shell tacos, or scatter it over nachos.
* Shepherd's Pie (or "Shepherdless" Pie): Use it as the base layer, topped with a vegetable gravy and mashed potato crust.
* Lasagna: Layer it with pasta sheets, ricotta (or vegan alternative), and tomato sauce.
* Stuffed Bell Peppers: Mix with cooked rice and vegetables to create a hearty stuffing.




🥧 Ultimate "Beefy" Tofu Shepherd's Pie
Yield: Serves 4–6
Prep time: 20 minutes
Cook time: 1 hour
________________


📋 Ingredients
The Beefy Tofu Mince
* 450 g firm or extra-firm tofu (pressed to remove excess water)
* 3 tbsp soy sauce or tamari
* 1 ½ tsp Marmite (dissolved in 3 tbsp warm water)
* 1 tbsp tomato purée
* 1 ½ tbsp balsamic vinegar or red wine
* 1 ½ tbsp neutral oil or toasted sesame oil
* 1 ½ tbsp nutritional yeast
* 1 tsp garlic powder
* ½ tsp MSG (monosodium glutamate)
* ¾ tsp dried thyme
* Scant ½ tsp toasted, crushed fennel seed
* Scant ½ tsp ground cumin
* Generous pinch of ground clove or allspice
* 1 medium bay leaf, crumbled (or ground)
* ¾ tsp smoked paprika
* Generous amount of cracked black pepper
* ⅜ tsp liquid smoke (optional)
Potato Topping
* 600 g Maris Piper or King Edward potatoes
* 50 g vegan butter
* 75–100 ml unsweetened soy milk
* 2 tbsp nutritional yeast
* Salt and white pepper, to taste
The Veg & Gravy Base
* 2 tbsp olive oil (for frying)
* 1 large onion, diced
* 2 carrots, diced
* 2 celery sticks, diced
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 2 tbsp tomato purée
* 1 tbsp plain flour
* 250 ml vegetable stock
* 100 ml red wine (optional but highly recommended)
* 1 tbsp vegan Worcestershire sauce
* 1 tsp English mustard
* 1 tsp fresh thyme (or ½ tsp dried)
* ½ tsp rosemary
* 100 g frozen peas
________________


🍳 Method
1. Bake the Tofu Mince
Preheat your oven to 200°C (180°C fan / 400°F). Crumble the pressed tofu into "mince" using your hands. In a large bowl, dissolve the Marmite in the warm water, then whisk in the tomato purée, MSG, garlic powder, and all the other marinade ingredients. Add the crumbled tofu and toss until completely coated. Spread it out evenly on a lined baking tray and bake for 20–25 minutes, tossing halfway through, until darkened, chewy, and browned.
2. Make the Mash
While the tofu bakes, boil the potatoes until tender. Drain well and let the steam escape for a minute or two (this keeps the mash fluffy). Mash with the vegan butter, soy milk, and nutritional yeast. Season generously with salt and white pepper. Set aside.
3. Build the Gravy Base
In a large pan or pot, heat the 2 tbsp olive oil over medium heat. Fry the diced onion, carrot, and celery for about 10 minutes until softened. Add the minced garlic and cook for 1 minute until fragrant.
4. Thicken and Deglaze
Stir in the 2 tbsp of tomato purée and cook for 1 minute to darken it. Add the flour and cook for 30 seconds. Pour in the red wine, scraping the bottom of the pan to lift up any sticky bits, and let it reduce by about half.
5. Simmer and Combine
Pour in the vegetable stock, vegan Worcestershire sauce, English mustard, rosemary, and extra thyme. When the baked tofu mince is done, scrape it all off the baking tray directly into the gravy. Simmer gently for 5–10 minutes so the gravy thickens and the tofu absorbs the surrounding liquid. Fold in the frozen peas right at the end. Taste and adjust seasoning if necessary.
6. Assemble and Bake
Transfer the meaty filling to a 9x13-inch (or similarly sized) baking dish. (Leave the oven on at 200°C / 180°C fan). Spread the mashed potatoes evenly over the top. Rough the surface with a fork to create ridges. Bake for 25–30 minutes until the edges are bubbling. Grill (broil) for the last 3–5 minutes for an extra crispy, golden crust.

Smoky & Savory Vegan Deli Ham
This recipe creates a firm, sliceable, and incredibly flavorful seitan-based ham, perfect for sandwiches, charcuterie boards, or dicing into salads and pasta. The combination of smoky, savory, and umami flavors mimics traditional deli meat beautifully.
Ingredients
________________


For the Wet Blend:
* 1 tin (approx. 400g / 15 oz) cannellini beans, drained (reserve the liquid/aquafaba)
* The reserved aquafaba from the tin of beans
* 1 tsp miso paste
* 3 tbsp nutritional yeast
* 1 tbsp garlic granules
* 1 tbsp onion granules
* 1 tbsp smoked salt (or 1 tbsp regular salt + 1 tsp smoked paprika)
* 1 tsp MSG (optional, but recommended for umami depth)
* 1 tsp baking powder
* 2 tbsp tapioca starch
* 1 tbsp liquid smoke
* ¼ tsp red food colouring (liquid or powder)
For the Dough:
* 250g (approx. 2 cups) vital wheat gluten
________________


Instructions
1. Preheat and Prepare: Preheat your oven to 175°C (350°F). Lightly grease your deli press. (See notes if you don't have one).
2. Create the Flavor Base: In a blender, combine all the "Wet Blend" ingredients: the drained cannellini beans, reserved aquafaba, miso paste, nutritional yeast, garlic and onion granules, salt, MSG, baking powder, tapioca starch, liquid smoke, and red food colouring.
3. Blend Until Smooth: Blend on high speed until the mixture is completely smooth and uniform in color. Scrape down the sides of the blender as needed to ensure there are no lumps.
4. Form the Dough: Pour the smooth liquid blend into a large mixing bowl. Add all 250g of the vital wheat gluten at once. Use a sturdy spatula or your hands to mix until a shaggy, cohesive dough forms.
5. Knead: Turn the dough out onto a clean surface and knead for 3-5 minutes. The goal is to develop the gluten strands, making the dough firm and elastic. It should feel like a very tight muscle.
6. Shape and Press: Shape the dough into a compact log that fits snugly into your greased deli press. Secure the lid.
7. Bake: Place the deli press on a baking sheet and bake for 40 to 60 minutes. The most accurate way to check for doneness is with an instant-read thermometer. The internal temperature of the seitan should be around 85°C (185°F).
8. Cool Completely: This is a crucial step! Once baked, remove the press from the oven and let it cool on a wire rack completely before opening. Cooling under compression gives the ham its dense, sliceable texture. For best results, refrigerate the unopened press for a few hours or overnight.
9. Slice and Serve: Once fully chilled, open the press, remove your vegan ham, and slice it thinly for sandwiches or cube it for other recipes.
________________


Notes & Tips
* No Deli Press? No Problem! You can achieve a similar result by shaping the dough into a tight log. Wrap it securely in a piece of parchment paper, and then wrap that package very tightly in a sheet of aluminum foil. Twist the ends of the foil like a toffee wrapper to compact the log. Bake as directed.
* The Role of Baking Powder: This is a key ingredient for improving the final taste. It helps to neutralize the distinct "wheaty" or slightly metallic flavor that vital wheat gluten can sometimes have, resulting in a cleaner, more savory final product.
* Why Tapioca Starch? This helps to create a smoother, less "gummy" texture and adds a pleasant, sliceable chew.
* Storage: Store the finished vegan ham in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to a week. It also freezes exceptionally well.
* Flavor Boost: For a slightly "cured" or festive flavor, consider adding a pinch of ground cloves or allspice to the wet blend.

Soya Milk Vegan Burger Buns
Yields: 6 buns
Prep time: 20 minutes
Rise time: 1 hour 40 minutes
Bake time: 15-17 minutes
________________


Ingredients:
* 300g all-purpose flour (approx. 9.7% protein)
* 6g active dry yeast (just under a standard 7g packet)
* 180ml soya milk, warmed to lukewarm (plus extra for brushing)
* 25ml vegetable oil
* 15g sugar
* 5g salt
________________


Instructions:
1. Activate Yeast & Combine Ingredients: In a large bowl, whisk together 200g of the flour and all of the active dry yeast. In a separate bowl or jug, combine the warm soya milk, vegetable oil, sugar, and salt.
2. Form the Dough: Pour the wet mixture into the flour/yeast mixture and stir until a shaggy dough forms. Gradually add the remaining 100g of flour, mixing until the dough comes together and pulls away from the sides of the bowl.
3. Knead: Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface. Knead for 8-10 minutes until it becomes smooth, soft, and elastic.
4. First Rise (Bulk Fermentation): Place the dough in a lightly greased bowl, cover it with a damp cloth or plastic wrap, and let it rise in a warm, draft-free place for about 1 hour, or until doubled in size.
5. Divide and Shape: Gently punch down the dough to release the air. Divide it into 6 equal portions (about 90g each). Shape each portion into a tight ball by tucking the edges underneath.
6. Second Rise (Proofing): Place the shaped buns on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper, leaving space between them. Cover loosely and let them rise for another 40 minutes until they are puffy.
7. Preheat and Bake: Preheat your oven to 190°C (375°F).
8. Finish: Just before baking, gently brush the tops of the buns with a little extra soya milk.
9. Bake: Bake for 15-17 minutes, or until the tops are a deep golden brown and the buns sound hollow when tapped on the bottom.
10. Cool: Transfer the buns immediately to a wire rack to cool completely before slicing. This prevents the bottoms from becoming soggy.
________________


Baker's Analysis & Notes
This is a fantastic base recipe. Here’s a breakdown of why it works so well:
* Hydration: The hydration level (liquid to flour ratio) is 180ml / 300g = 60%. This is a perfect starting point for a soft, manageable dough that isn't too sticky for a beginner.
* Enrichment: The oil and sugar are "enrichments." The oil tenderizes the crumb, making the buns soft and rich. The sugar adds a hint of sweetness, helps with browning, and provides food for the yeast to make the rise more vigorous.
* Flour Choice: Using all-purpose flour with a moderate protein content (9.7%) is ideal for soft burger buns. A high-protein bread flour would result in a chewier, more bread-like texture, which you may or may not want.
* Technique: Holding back some of the flour (adding 100g at the end) is a professional technique. It allows you to control the dough's consistency, as different flours absorb water differently. You only add enough to make the dough workable, preventing it from becoming too dry or dense.
* Vegan "Egg" Wash: Brushing with soya milk before baking is a great vegan alternative to an egg wash. It helps achieve a nice golden-brown color and a slight sheen.
Tips for Perfect Buns
* Yeast Health: To ensure your yeast is active, you can "bloom" it first. Add the yeast and sugar to the warm soya milk and let it sit for 5-10 minutes. If it becomes foamy, your yeast is alive and well.
* The Windowpane Test: To check if your dough is kneaded enough, take a small piece and gently stretch it between your fingers. If you can stretch it thin enough to see light through it without it tearing, the gluten is well-developed.
* Shaping is Key: For a tall, rounded bun, shaping is crucial. Roll each portion into a tight ball, creating surface tension. This encourages the bun to rise up instead of spreading out in the oven.
* The Poke Test: To see if your buns have risen enough during the second rise, gently poke one with a floured finger. If the indentation springs back immediately, it needs more time. If it springs back slowly and leaves a small indent, it's ready to bake. If it collapses, it's over-proofed.
Variations & Substitutions
* Add Toppings: After brushing with soya milk, sprinkle the buns with sesame seeds, poppy seeds, flaky sea salt, or everything bagel seasoning.
* Whole Wheat Buns: For a heartier bun, substitute 50-75g of the all-purpose flour with whole wheat flour. You may need to add an extra tablespoon of soya milk, as whole wheat flour is thirstier.
* Different Milks: Oat milk or almond milk will work just as well as soya milk.
* Richer Bun: For a richer, softer bun, you can substitute the vegetable oil with an equal amount of melted vegan butter.

Water-Based Vegan Burger Buns (Makes 6 x 110g Buns)
This recipe is scaled to produce a total dough weight of approximately 665g, which divides perfectly into six substantial buns.
Ingredients:
* 375g all-purpose flour
* 6.5g active dry yeast (most of one standard 7g packet)
* 225ml water, warm (plus extra for the sugar wash)
* 33ml vegetable oil
* 20g sugar
* 6g salt
Instructions:
1. Activate Yeast & Combine: In a large bowl, whisk together 250g of the flour and all of the active dry yeast. In a separate bowl or jug, combine the warm water, vegetable oil, sugar, and salt, and stir until the sugar and salt are dissolved.
2. Form the Dough: Pour the wet mixture into the flour/yeast mixture and stir until a shaggy dough forms. Gradually add the remaining 125g of flour, mixing until the dough comes together and pulls away from the sides of the bowl.
3. Knead: Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface. Knead for 8-10 minutes until it becomes smooth, soft, and elastic.
4. First Rise: Place the dough in a lightly greased bowl, cover it, and let it rise in a warm, draft-free place for about 1 hour, or until doubled in size.
5. Divide and Shape: Gently punch down the dough. Divide it into 6 equal portions (they will weigh approximately 110g each). Shape each portion into a tight ball.
6. Second Rise: Place the shaped buns on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper, leaving plenty of space between them. Cover loosely and let them rise for another 40-50 minutes until they are puffy.
7. Preheat Oven: Preheat your oven to 190°C (375°F).
8. Finish & Bake: Just before baking, gently brush the tops of the buns with the Optional Sugar Wash (see note below). Bake for 17-20 minutes, or until the tops are a light golden brown and the buns sound hollow when tapped on the bottom.
9. Cool: Transfer the buns immediately to a wire rack to cool completely.
________________


Important Note on Using Water Instead of Milk
Substituting water for milk is a perfectly fine adjustment, but it will change the final bun in three key ways. Here’s what to expect:
1. Flavor: The bun will have a simpler, cleaner, more "bread-like" flavor, rather than the richer taste that the fats and sugars in milk provide.
2. Texture: The crumb will be slightly less soft and tender, and a little bit chewier.
3. Browning: This is the biggest difference. Milk contains sugars (lactose) that help the crust achieve a deep, even golden-brown color. A water-based dough will result in a much paler crust.
To get a better color and a slight sheen on your water-based buns, use this optional sugar wash:
Optional Sugar Wash: In a small cup, dissolve 1 teaspoon of sugar into 1 tablespoon of warm water. Brush this mixture gently over the buns right before they go into the oven. The sugar will help the crust caramelize and achieve a more appealing golden color.

The Definitive Umami Burger (v5.0)
This recipe is optimized for a convincing meaty texture and a deep, complex umami flavor profile. It leverages modern binders and flavor layering for a truly gourmet result.
Yields: 8-10 substantial patties
Prep Time: 30 minutes
Chill Time: 90+ minutes
Cook Time: 8-10 minutes per batch
________________


Ingredients
Part A: The Sautéed Flavor Base
* 2 tbsp olive oil
* 1 large red onion, finely chopped
* 1 whole red bell pepper, finely chopped
* 6 cloves garlic, minced
* 2 tbsp tomato puree
Part B: The Umami Liquid
* 2 tbsp tamari (or soy sauce)
* 2 tbsp vegan Worcestershire sauce
* 2 tbsp red or white miso paste
* 2 tsp Marmite
* 2 tsp light brown sugar (or maple syrup)
Part C: The Dry Mix & Binders
* 3-4 tbsp dried porcini powder
* 3/4 cup (90g) vital wheat gluten
* 1/2 cup (40g) nutritional yeast
* 2 tsp smoked paprika
* 1 tsp black pepper
* 1/2 - 3/4 tsp MSG
Part D: The Bulk & Final Binder
* 2 (15-oz / 425g) cans red kidney or cannellini beans, rinsed and oven-dried until matte
* 1 cup (120g) toasted walnuts
* 1/2 cup chopped fresh coriander
* 4 tsp (approx. 16g) Methylcellulose F50
* 1/2 cup (120ml) ice-cold water
________________


Instructions
1. Sauté the Flavor Base: Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and red pepper and cook, stirring occasionally, for 10-15 minutes until very soft, jammy, and the moisture has cooked off. Add the minced garlic and tomato puree and cook for 1 more minute until fragrant. Scrape into a large mixing bowl and let it cool completely.
2. Prepare the Liquids: In a small bowl, whisk together all ingredients from Part B (The Umami Liquid) until the sugar, miso, and Marmite are fully dissolved. In a separate, slightly larger bowl, whisk the Methylcellulose powder into the ice-cold water until smooth. Pour the Umami Liquid into the Methocel water and whisk to combine.
3. Process the Bulk Ingredients: Place the toasted walnuts in a food processor and pulse until coarsely ground. Add the oven-dried beans. Pulse 4-6 times ONLY. The goal is a coarse, crumbly mixture with visible pieces of bean and nut, not a smooth paste.

7. Shape the Patties: Divide the chilled mixture into 8-10 equal portions. With damp hands, gently shape each portion into a patty about ½ to ¾ inch thick. Press a shallow "dimple" into the center of each patty to help it cook flat.
8. Cook: Heat a tablespoon of neutral oil in a cast-iron or non-stick skillet over medium-high heat. Cook the patties for 4-5 minutes per side, until a deep, dark brown crust forms and the patties feel firm to the touch. Cook in batches to avoid crowding the pan. Serve immediately.

Vegan Butter Tofu (Tofu Makhani)
This recipe has three main parts: preparing and marinating the tofu, cooking the tofu until golden, and creating a rich, velvety Makhani sauce.
* Prep time: 20 minutes
* Marinating time: 1–4 hours (minimum 1 hour)
* Cook time: 35–40 minutes
* Serves: 3–4
________________


Ingredients
Part 1: Tofu & Marinade
* 450 g extra-firm tofu, pressed for at least 30 minutes
* 45 ml (3 Tbsp) plain unsweetened vegan yogurt
* 15 ml (1 Tbsp) lemon juice
* 22.5 ml (1½ Tbsp) ginger-garlic paste
* 15 ml (1 Tbsp) neutral oil
* 5 ml (1 tsp) Kashmiri chilli powder (or ¾ tsp sweet paprika + ¼ tsp cayenne)
* 5 ml (1 tsp) garam masala
* 2.5 ml (½ tsp) turmeric
* 5 ml (1 tsp) salt
* 30 ml (2 Tbsp) nutritional yeast (optional)
Part 2: Makhani Sauce

* 120 ml hot water
* 60 g vegan butter, divided
* Optional Whole Spices: 3 green cardamom pods, 2–3 cm cinnamon stick, 2 whole cloves
Sauce Ingredients:
* 22.5 ml (1½ Tbsp) ginger-garlic paste
* 5 ml (1 tsp) Kashmiri chilli powder
* 2.5 ml (½ tsp) turmeric
* 800 g crushed tomatoes or tomato passata
* 5 ml (1 tsp) salt (plus more to taste)
* 7.5 ml (1½ tsp) sugar or maple syrup
* 5 ml (1 tsp) garam masala
* 15 ml (1 Tbsp) dried fenugreek leaves (kasuri methi)
* 60–120 ml full-fat unsweetened plant milk or vegan cream
To Serve
* Fresh coriander, chopped
* Steamed basmati rice
* Vegan naan
________________


Instructions
Stage 1: Prepare and Marinate the Tofu
1. Prepare: After pressing, tear the tofu into bite-sized irregular pieces. The rough edges hold the marinade better.
2. Make the marinade: In a large bowl, whisk together the yogurt, lemon juice, ginger-garlic paste, oil, chilli powder, garam masala, turmeric, salt, and nutritional yeast until smooth.
3. Marinate: Add the tofu and gently toss until evenly coated. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour (recommended: 2–4 hours).
Stage 2: Cook the Tofu
* Option A (Pan-fry): Heat 1–2 Tbsp oil in a skillet over medium-high heat. Cook for 3–4 minutes per side until golden brown and slightly charred.
* Option B (Air Fry - Recommended): Preheat to 200°C. Air fry for 12–15 minutes, shaking halfway through.
* Option C (Bake): Bake at 200°C for 20–25 minutes, turning halfway through.
Stage 3: Make the Makhani Sauce
1. Cashew cream: Soak cashews in hot water for 15 minutes. Blend the cashews and soaking liquid until completely smooth.
2. Sauté: Melt 45 g (3 Tbsp) vegan butter in a pot over medium heat. Add whole spices and cook for 30–45 seconds until fragrant.
3. Aromatics: Add ginger-garlic paste; cook for 1 minute while stirring.
4. Bloom: Add chilli powder and turmeric; stir for 20–30 seconds (do not burn).
5. Simmer: Add tomatoes and salt. Simmer on low, partially covered, for 15–20 minutes until the raw tomato taste is gone.
6. Cream: Stir in the cashew cream and simmer for 5 minutes.
7. Blend: Remove whole spices. Blend the sauce until completely smooth (using a standing or immersion blender).
8. Finish: Return to low heat. Stir in sugar/syrup, garam masala, crushed kasuri methi, the remaining 15 g butter, and the plant cream. Heat gently for 1–2 minutes (do not boil). Taste and adjust seasoning.
Stage 4 & 5: Assemble and Serve
* Gently fold the cooked tofu into the sauce and simmer for 2–3 minutes.
* Divide into bowls, garnish with fresh coriander, and serve with basmati rice and vegan naan.

Tips
* Kasuri Methi: Highly recommended for an authentic, restaurant-style flavour.
* Spice level: Kashmiri chilli powder provides colour and mild heat. Adjust cayenne if substituting.
* Make-ahead: The sauce can be made 1–2 days ahead; flavours improve overnight.
* Storage: Leftovers keep well refrigerated for 3–4 days.

Cauliflower Steaks with Butter Bean Purée & Chimichurri
Serves: 2
________________


Ingredients
The Cauliflower
* 2 thick cauliflower steaks (cut from the center of 1 large head)
* 1 tbsp smoked paprika
* 3 garlic cloves, bashed
* 2 tbsp butter (use vegan butter if preferred)
* Olive oil, salt, and pepper
* Reserved: Cauliflower leaves and stems (finely chopped)
The Chimichurri
* 1 large bunch flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
* 1 garlic clove, minced
* 1 red chili, finely diced (de-seeded for less heat)
* 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
* 100ml extra virgin olive oil
* Salt and pepper to taste
The Butter Bean Purée
* 700g jarred butter beans (keep the liquid/aquafaba)
* 1 lemon, juiced
* 1 garlic clove
* 2 tbsp nutritional yeast
* Salt and pepper
________________


Method
1. Make the Chimichurri
In a small bowl, combine the chopped parsley, diced chili, and minced garlic. Stir in the red wine vinegar. While stirring, slowly pour in the extra virgin olive oil. Season with salt and pepper.
* Tip: Let this sit while you cook everything else; the flavors develop significantly over 20–30 minutes.
2. Prepare the Butter Bean Purée
Add the beans (and their liquid), lemon juice, garlic, nutritional yeast, salt, and pepper into a high-speed blender. Blitz until completely smooth.
* Tip: If it’s too thick, add a splash of water or more lemon juice. If you want it warm, you can heat it gently in a small saucepan just before serving.
3. Prep the Cauliflower
Cut two 1-inch thick "steaks" from the center of the cauliflower (where the core holds the florets together). Season both sides generously with salt, pepper, and smoked paprika. Drizzle with oil and massage the spices into the nooks and crannies.
4. Sear and Baste
Heat a large frying pan over medium-high heat with 2 tbsp olive oil. Place the steaks in the pan and press down firmly to ensure even browning. Fry for 4–5 minutes until deeply golden.
Flip the steaks. Add the butter and the bashed garlic cloves to the pan. As the butter melts and foams, use a spoon to continuously pour it over the cauliflower (basting) until the steak is tender but still has a "bite." Remove the steaks and let them rest.
5. The Zero-Waste Garnish
In the same pan (with the leftover cauliflower butter), toss in the finely chopped cauliflower leaves and stems. Sauté for 2–3 minutes with a pinch of salt until they are crispy and golden.
6. Assembly
Spread a generous swoosh of the warm butter bean purée onto each plate. Place the cauliflower steak on top. Spoon over the vibrant chimichurri and finish with a sprinkle of the crispy leaves and stems.
________________


A Few Tips for Success:
* The "Steak" Cut: Don't throw away the florets that fall off! Roast them on a tray at the same time and serve them on the side or save them for a salad tomorrow.
* Why Jarred Beans? Jarred beans (like Bold Bean Co or similar) are usually much creamier and come in a thick, starchy liquid that makes the purée silkier than canned beans.
* Heat Control: If the paprika starts to smell burnt or looks too dark, turn the heat down slightly. Paprika has a high sugar content and can go from "smoky" to "bitter" quickly.

Ingredients
* 400g plain flour

* 1 teaspoon sugar

Method

The Beefy Tofu Mince

Potato Topping
* 1.2 kg Maris Piper or King Edward potatoes

🍳 Method
1. Bake the Tofu Mince
Preheat your oven to 200°C (180°C fan / 400°F). Crumble the pressed tofu into "mince" using your hands.
